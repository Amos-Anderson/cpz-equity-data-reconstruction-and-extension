  1. PROJECT IDENTITY
================================================================================

  1.1  OBJECTIVE
  --------------
  Build an industry-grade long/short fundamental factor investing pipeline for
  US equities, end-to-end, with mathematical rigor at every stage and full
  academic documentation. The final strategy is paper-traded on Alpaca.

  The pipeline must satisfy three simultaneous standards:
    (a) Academic rigor — derivations, citations, statistical discipline
    (b) Industry-grade engineering — modular, tested, reproducible
    (c) Pedagogical clarity — usable as a teaching reference

  1.2  STAKEHOLDER GOALS
  ----------------------
    - Personal investing capability (trade the strategy on own account)
    - Resume / portfolio artifact for institutional quant roles
    - Teaching material for future use

  1.3  DELIVERABLES
  -----------------
    (a) Standalone Stage 0 data reconstruction package
    (b) Python package `factor_pipeline/` with modular architecture
    (c) Jupyter notebooks, one per stage, that import from the package
    (d) LaTeX research report with full mathematical documentation
    (e) Paper trading deployment on Alpaca
    (f) Test suite covering core math operations
    (g) Configuration artifacts (YAML) for each stage
    (h) Stage 4 leaderboard comparing 4 signal-combination approaches

  1.4  SUCCESS CRITERIA
  ---------------------
    Technical:
      - All 11 stages implemented, tested, documented
      - Reproducible: single command runs the full pipeline end-to-end
      - PIT-correct: no look-ahead bias in any stage
      - Stage 4 leaderboard cleanly identifies best combination method
      - Backtest passes out-of-sample with positive Sharpe in test holdout
      - Paper trading deployed and running on Alpaca

    Academic:
      - LaTeX report cites every method used
      - Every non-trivial formula derived or derivation referenced
      - Stage-by-stage math framework teachable end-to-end
      - Stage 0 reconstruction validated against CPZ AND Ken French data
      - Stage 4 four-method comparison rigorously executed

    Practical:
      - Strategy survives a pessimistic transaction cost assumption
      - Capacity analysis shows meaningful tradable AUM
      - Risk attribution is clean (factor exposures controlled, idio residual)

================================================================================
  3. CORE PHILOSOPHICAL PRINCIPLES 
================================================================================

  3.1  MATHEMATICAL RIGOR FIRST
  -----------------------------
  At each stage, before writing any code, the full mathematical framework
  must be built and explained:
    - All relevant formulas with derivations
    - All assumptions stated explicitly
    - All references cited
    - Alternatives considered and why we chose what we did
    - Criticism of the chosen approach (where it might fail)

  Code is a translation of math into runnable form. If the math is unclear
  or incomplete, the code cannot be trusted regardless of test pass rate.

  3.2  DEEPLY PEDAGOGICAL
  -----------------------
  Every stage walks through:
    (a) Intuition: why we're doing this in plain language
    (b) Mathematical formalization: the precise statement
    (c) Derivation: where the formulas come from
    (d) Alternatives: what other approaches exist
    (e) Criticism: what could go wrong, what assumptions we're making
    (f) Reference: where to read more (papers, textbooks)

    3.3  EMPIRICAL COMPARISON OVER A PRIORI COMMITMENT
  --------------------------------------------------
  Where multiple defensible methods exist, we IMPLEMENT MULTIPLE METHODS
  and compare them on consistent metrics. The most prominent application:
  Stage 4's four-method comparison (statistical filtering, SVD/PCA,
  regularized regression, Risk Premium PCA).

  We do NOT a priori commit to a method based on aesthetic preference,
  literature familiarity, or intuition. We EXECUTE the comparison and let
  the data choose.

  3.4  NO POST-HOC FILTERING
  --------------------------
  Decision rules (significance thresholds, robustness criteria, weighting
  schemes) are LOCKED IN before computation. Whatever number of factors
  pass our criterion is the number; we do not adjust thresholds to hit
  a target count.

  3.5  HONEST RESULTS REPORTING
  -----------------------------
  Whatever results emerge from the pipeline are reported as-is, with
  explanations rather than apologies. Surprising findings are valuable
  if methodologically sound.

  STAGE 2 — FACTOR CONSTRUCTION
================================================================================

  Objective
  ---------
  Build alpha-signal factors from the 46 raw characteristics with proper
  neutralization, standardization.
  Output is a set of cross-sectionally normalized factor signals per
  stock-month.

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - Rank-normalization (CPZ convention): t_i = (rank_i - 0.5) / N - 0.5
    - Cross-sectional z-scoring as alternative
    - Sector neutralization via cross-sectional OLS:
          z = alpha + beta_sector * D_sector + epsilon
    - Joint sector + size neutralization for value/profitability factors
    - Rank-duplicate detection and dropping (Spearman > 0.99)
    - Factor family taxonomy: value, profitability, investment, momentum,
      risk, other (6 families)

  Cross-family correlations are PRESERVED by design because cross-family relations carry economic content.
  Multicollinearity between families is the responsibility of Stage 4
  combination methods to handle.

  Academic references

    Fama & French (1992, 1993, 2015) - value, profitability, investment
    Novy-Marx (2013) - gross profitability
    Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) - momentum
    Sloan (1996) - accruals
    Frazzini & Pedersen (2014) - betting against beta
    Daniel & Titman (1997) - characteristics vs covariances

================================================================================
  STAGE 3 — FACTOR VALIDATION (REPOSITIONED IN V2)
================================================================================

  Objective change in V2
  ----------------------
  v1 used Stage 3 as a "factor gate": only factors passing all 5 BH-FDR
  tests proceeded to Stage 4. This led to post-hoc threshold-hunting and
  arbitrary cutoffs.

  v2 repositions Stage 3 as a DIAGNOSTIC layer that produces statistics
  on every factor, informing each of Stage 4's four approaches without
  filtering factors out a priori. All 41 factors proceed to Stage 4;
  each Stage 4 approach uses Stage 3's diagnostics differently.

  Mathematical content 
  -------------------------------------
    - Information Coefficient: monthly Spearman rank correlation
    - Newey-West HAC standard errors:
          Var(sqrt(T) * IC_bar) = gamma_0 + 2 sum_{l=1}^{L} w_l gamma_l
      with Bartlett kernel w_l = 1 - l/(L+1)
    - Characteristic-based Fama-MacBeth (NOT classical 1973 two-step):
          r_{i,t+1} = alpha + lambda * z_{i,t,f} + epsilon  per month
      Distinguished from FM(1973) two-step time-series-betas approach
    - GRS test (Gibbons-Ross-Shanken 1989) for joint alpha significance
    - Benjamini-Hochberg FDR for multiple testing
    - Harvey-Liu-Zhu (2016) haircuts for asset-pricing factor mining
    - Quintile/decile portfolio sorts: monotonicity slope test
    - IC decay profile across horizons (1, 3, 12 months)
    - Spanning tests: factor adds alpha over FF5 + UMD?
    - Look-ahead audit: same-month vs next-month correlation diagnostic
    - Pre/post-1986 sub-period stability check

  Output (CHANGED IN V2)
  ----------------------
  v1 output: triage_ledger.parquet with PROCEED/REVISE/STOP per factor.
  v2 output: factor_diagnostics.parquet with full statistics per factor.
    No PROCEED/STOP labels. All factors pass through to Stage 4.

  Stage 4 will use the diagnostics differently per approach:
    - Approach 1 (Statistical filtering): filter on diagnostics
    - Approach 2 (SVD/PCA): use full factor set, transform to PCs
    - Approach 3 (Regularized regression): use full factor set, let penalty work
    - Approach 4 (Risk Premium PCA): use full factor set, follow LP 2018 algo

  Academic references
  -------------------
    Fama & MacBeth (1973) - cross-sectional regression
    Newey & West (1987) - HAC covariance
    Gibbons, Ross & Shanken (1989) - GRS alpha test
    Benjamini & Hochberg (1995) - FDR procedure
    Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) - t-statistic haircuts
    McLean & Pontiff (2016) - post-publication factor decay
    Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) - replication of factor anomalies

================================================================================
  STAGE 4 — SIGNAL COMBINATION & MODEL SELECTION (FULLY REDESIGNED IN V2)
================================================================================

  v1 vs v2
  --------
  v1: pick a single combination method (Ridge), fit it, hand off result.
      Outcome: Ridge composite IC ≈ 0 but spread Sharpe = 1.0; method
      didn't generalize as expected.
  v2: implement FOUR approaches, compare on consistent metrics, leaderboard
      identifies the winner that proceeds to Stage 5.

  THE FOUR APPROACHES (with mathematical content per approach)

  Approach 1: Statistical Filtering + Composite Weighting
  -------------------------------------------------------
  Use Stage 3 diagnostics to score factors, then composite the top set.

  Math:
    - Composite score per factor: weighted vote across 6 tests
        score_f = w1 * 1{IC_h1 sig} + w2 * 1{IC_h3 sig} + ... + w6 * 1{spread sig}
    - Robust set: factors with score >= threshold
    - IC-weighted composite: alpha_i = sum_{f in R} (IC_f / sigma_f) * z_{i,f}
    - Equal-weight composite as baseline
    - Mean-variance optimal weights with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage

  References:
    Asness, Frazzini, Pedersen (2019) - quality factor combination
    Ledoit & Wolf (2003) - shrinkage estimation
    Grinold & Kahn (2000) - optimal IC-weighting

  Approach 2: SVD/PCA Orthogonalization
  -------------------------------------
  Transform 41 factors into orthogonal principal components, then combine.

  Math:
    - SVD of factor matrix: Z = U S V'
    - Principal components: Z V_k (top k eigenvectors)
    - Variance explained: sigma_k^2 / sum(sigma_j^2)
    - PC-based composite: alpha_i = sum_k w_k * PC_{i,k}
        where w_k can be: equal, IC-weighted, or learned
    - Selection of k: scree plot, parallel analysis, fixed fraction
    - Scaling and centering choices: critical to interpretation
    - Stability of PCs over rolling windows
    - Comparison to non-orthogonalized starting point

  Why this addresses the multicollinearity issue: PCs are orthogonal by
  construction, eliminating the BEME-Q -0.86 correlation problem at the
  combination level rather than relying on Ridge to handle it via penalty.

  References:
    Connor & Korajczyk (1986, 1988) - PCA in asset pricing
    Bai & Ng (2002) - determining the number of factors
    Stock & Watson (2002) - dynamic factor models

  Approach 3: Regularized Regression
  ----------------------------------
  Apply Ridge / Lasso / ElasticNet to the original factor matrix.

  Math:
    - Ridge: argmin ||y - Xw||^2 + lambda ||w||_2^2
    - Lasso: argmin ||y - Xw||^2 + lambda ||w||_1
    - ElasticNet: argmin ||y - Xw||^2 + lambda(alpha ||w||_1 + (1-alpha) ||w||_2^2 / 2)
    - Walk-forward CV with expanding window (or purged + embargo)
    - Lambda selection grid: log-spaced, wide enough that CV doesn't pick boundary
    - Annual refit during OOS evaluation
    - Standardization choices and their consequences

  Lessons from v1 Ridge attempt:
    - CV may pick smallest lambda in grid → grid was too narrow → check
    - CV scores diverging from OOS scores → indicates overfit → consider purged CV
    - OLS-like Ridge produces unstable weights → expected behavior

  References:
    Hoerl & Kennard (1970) - Ridge regression origin
    Tibshirani (1996) - Lasso
    Zou & Hastie (2005) - ElasticNet
    Lopez de Prado (2018) - Purged CV
    Gu, Kelly, Xiu (2020) - empirical asset pricing via ML

  Approach 4: Risk Premium PCA (Lettau-Pelger 2018)
  -------------------------------------------------
  Modern method that estimates latent factors maximizing risk premium
  variance, not just total variance. To be implemented when the
  Lettau-Pelger paper is provided.

  Math (preview, full derivation when paper is in hand):
    - Standard PCA finds directions of max VARIANCE in returns
    - LP 2018 finds directions of max RISK PREMIUM (mean) in returns
    - Optimization: max_v (v' E[r])^2 / (v' Cov(r) v)
    - Intuition: traditional PCA gives the loudest factors; LP gives
      the most COMPENSATED factors
    - Often produces fewer but more economically meaningful components

  Reference:
    Lettau & Pelger (2018) - Estimating Latent Asset-Pricing Factors
    [Paper to be provided when Stage 4 implementation begins]

  STAGE 4 LEADERBOARD AND METHOD SELECTION

  Each of the 4 approaches produces a composite alpha signal evaluated
  on consistent metrics:
    - Mean cross-sectional IC over OOS period
    - IC Sharpe (IC mean / IC std, annualized)
    - Decile-spread mean monthly return
    - Decile-spread Sharpe (annualized)
    - Decile monotonicity slope
    - Realized cumulative spread return

  Leaderboard ranks methods on each metric. Selection rules:
    Primary: highest decile-spread Sharpe (this is what trading targets)
    Tiebreaker: highest IC Sharpe (proxies signal stability)

  Diagnostic plots: each method's monthly IC time series,
                    each method's cumulative spread return,
                    method ranking heatmap across metrics.

  The winner's composite signal becomes the Stage 5 input. Other methods
  archived for sensitivity analysis.

  Mathematical content (deep)

    - Walk-forward CV with expanding window
    - Newey-West HAC for monthly IC inference
    - Decile-spread Sharpe with monthly compounding
    - Multiple-method comparison statistics

    - Equal-weight and PC1 composites as benchmarks

================================================================================
  STAGE 5 — RISK MODELING
================================================================================

  Objective
  ---------
  Build a cross-sectional fundamental factor risk model that decomposes
  portfolio risk into factor exposures and idiosyncratic components. Extend
  with GARCH-NIG innovations.

  5A. Baseline BARRA-style risk model

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - Structural model: r_{i,t} = sum_k B_{i,k,t-1} f_{k,t} + e_{i,t}
    - Cross-sectional weighted least squares for factor returns f_{k,t}
    - Factor covariance estimation F_t: EWMA, shrinkage, GARCH
    - Idiosyncratic variance D_t: EWMA per stock
    - Full covariance: Sigma_t = B_t F_t B_t' + D_t
    - Newey-West for serial correlation in factor returns
    - Bayesian shrinkage for unstable factors

  5B. GARCH-NIG extension

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - ARMA(p,q) mean for each factor time series
    - GARCH(1,1) on standardized innovations:
          sigma_{k,t}^2 = omega + alpha * eps_{k,t-1}^2 + beta * sigma_{k,t-1}^2
    - NIG distribution for innovations z_{k,t} ~ NIG(alpha, beta, mu, delta)
    - DCC-GARCH (Engle 2002) for correlation dynamics
    - Multivariate NIG copula for joint factor distribution
    - Per-stock idiosyncratic GARCH on residuals

  5C. Model validation

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - Bias statistic: BARRA's standard model test
    - VaR backtesting: Kupiec POF, Christoffersen independence
    - Risk attribution: decompose realized portfolio variance
    - Out-of-sample comparison: baseline vs GARCH-NIG

  Academic references
  -------------------
    Rosenberg (1974) - BARRA factor model origin
    MSCI BARRA USE4 Handbook - structural risk models
    Engle (2002) - DCC-GARCH
    Bollerslev (1986) - GARCH
    Barndorff-Nielsen (1997) - NIG distribution
    van der Weide (2002) - GO-GARCH
    Menn & Rachev (2005) - NIG in finance
    Kupiec (1995) - VaR coverage tests
    Christoffersen (1998) - conditional VaR tests

  ---------
  Model trading costs realistically: spread, market impact, borrow.

  Mathematical content

    - Market impact (square-root law):

    - Opportunity cost of unfilled orders

    D'Avolio (2002) - stock loan market

================================================================================
  STAGE 7 — PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION
================================================================================

  Objective
  ---------
  Turn alpha forecasts + risk model into portfolio weights.

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - Mean-variance optimization:
          max_w alpha'w - (lambda/2) w' Sigma w - TC(w, w_prev)
      s.t. constraints
    - Constraint set:
        - Dollar neutrality
        - Gross leverage cap
        - Position size cap (GameStop lesson)
        - Sector exposure limits
        - Beta neutrality
        - Turnover cap
        - ADV participation
        - Factor exposure limits
    - Transaction cost penalty: linear (spread) + quadratic (impact)
    - QP solver: CVXPY backend (OSQP free; MOSEK if licensed)
    - Warm-starting from previous period
    - Dual values for Lagrangian interpretation

  Academic references
  -------------------
    Markowitz (1952) - mean-variance foundation
    Black & Litterman (1992) - blended views with prior
    Grinold & Kahn (2000) - Active Portfolio Management
    Almgren & Chriss (2000) - optimal execution
    Clarke, de Silva & Thorley (2002) - long-short construction

  Deliverables

================================================================================
  STAGE 8 — BACKTEST ENGINE
================================================================================

  Objective
  ---------
  Simulate full strategy P&L with realistic execution, costs, borrow,
  delisting handling. Two variants: vectorized (fast) and event-driven
  (realistic).

  Mathematical content
  --------------------
    - Path integral of portfolio returns with costs:
          PnL_t = sum_i w_{i,t-1} r_{i,t} - sum_i TC(w_{i,t}, w_{i,t-1})
    - Slippage modeling: actual fill price vs decision price
    - Delisting-month handling
    - Walk-forward with purged CV
    - Sharpe, Information Ratio, drawdown, turnover, capacity
    - Deflated Sharpe Ratio for multi-trial backtest correction

  Academic references
  -------------------
    Bailey & Lopez de Prado (2014) - deflated Sharpe ratio
    Harvey & Liu (2015) - backtest overfitting
    Lopez de Prado (2018) - Advances in FinML
